Abdullah Khalifa and His Intellectual Project: Idealist Trends in Arab-Islamic Philosophy
Introduction

Abdullah Khalifa's multi-volume work Idealist Trends in Arab-Islamic Philosophy constitutes one of his most significant intellectual achievements. Rather than serving merely as a history of Arab-Islamic philosophy, the series offers a comprehensive attempt to explain the emergence of religious, theological, and philosophical currents through their relationship to the social, economic, and political structures of Arab and Islamic societies.

Khalifa's project is characterized by a historical-sociological approach that bears a strong affinity to the tradition of historical materialism associated with Arab Marxist thinkers such as Hussein Muruwwa. Nevertheless, Khalifa maintains a considerable degree of intellectual independence, developing his own analytical framework for interpreting the evolution of Arab-Islamic thought.

Part I and Part II: The Foundational Volume

The first two parts, published together in a single volume of approximately six hundred pages, investigate the historical and intellectual preconditions that preceded the emergence of Islam and the later development of Arab-Islamic philosophy.

The Central Question

At the heart of these volumes lies a fundamental inquiry:

How was the Arab consciousness formed that ultimately produced Islam, theological schools, and later philosophical systems?

Consequently, Khalifa begins not with philosophers but with the social structure of pre-Islamic Arabia.

1. Arab Society Before Islam

The study examines:

Tribal social organization.
Pastoral and commercial economic structures.
The rise of Mecca as a major commercial center.
The role of Quraysh in unifying Arab interests.
Relations with the Byzantine and Persian empires.

The central argument is that religious ideas do not emerge in a historical vacuum; rather, they express profound social transformations.

2. Islam as a Historical Transformation

Khalifa interprets Islam not merely as a religious event but as:

A unifying project for the Arabs.
A transition from tribal solidarity ('asabiyyah) to the concept of the wider community (ummah).
A new formulation of political and social consciousness.

Within this framework, he analyzes:

The Prophetic state.
The Rashidun Caliphate.
The Islamic conquests.
Class and social transformations resulting from the expansion of the state.
3. The Origins of Islamic Sects

The author proceeds to examine the emergence of:

The Kharijites.
The Shi'ites.
The Murji'ites.
The Mu'tazilites.
Early Sunni tendencies.

These movements are interpreted as reflections of social and political conflicts rather than purely doctrinal disagreements.

4. The Formation of Rational Theology

A central concern of these volumes is the development of kalam (Islamic theology), particularly debates concerning:

Determinism and free will.
Divine justice.
Human freedom.
Reason and revelation.

Khalifa pays special attention to the Mu'tazilites, regarding them as the first major rationalist movement in Islamic intellectual history.

Academic Significance

The first two volumes can be regarded as:

A sociology of Islamic thought.
A study of the formation of early Arab consciousness.
An essential foundation for understanding later Arab-Islamic philosophy.

Their purpose is not merely to describe ideas but to explain the historical conditions that produced them.

Part III: The Formation of Arab-Islamic Philosophy from al-Farabi to Ibn Rushd

The third volume examines the emergence and maturation of Arab-Islamic philosophy through its major representatives, from al-Farabi to Ibn Rushd (Averroes).

At this stage, Khalifa moves from historical preparation to the study of philosophers themselves.

Al-Farabi

Al-Farabi is presented as:

The true founder of systematic Arab-Islamic philosophy.
The philosopher who attempted to reconcile Plato and Aristotle.
The author of the theory of the Virtuous City.

Khalifa focuses on:

The relationship between philosophy and political authority.
The concept of the philosopher-ruler.
The political dimension of Farabian philosophy.
Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

Ibn Sina represents a more advanced stage of philosophical idealism.

Khalifa discusses:

The distinction between essence and existence.
The concept of the Necessary Being.
The human soul.
Knowledge and illumination.

He regards Ibn Sina's work as the highest expression of metaphysical system-building within Islamic philosophy.

Esoteric and Isma'ili Currents

The volume also examines:

Isma'ili writings.
The Brethren of Purity (Ikhwan al-Safa').
Gnostic and esoteric tendencies.

These currents are analyzed as important components of idealist thought in the Arab-Islamic intellectual tradition.

Al-Ghazali

Al-Ghazali is treated as a pivotal figure.

The study addresses:

His critique of the philosophers.
His concept of certainty.
Sufism.
The relationship between reason and revelation.

Khalifa does not view him simply as an opponent of philosophy but as the creator of a new philosophical and spiritual orientation.

Ibn Bajja and Ibn Tufayl

Within Andalusian philosophy, attention is directed to:

Philosophical solitude.
The individual's quest for truth.
The reconciliation of reason and religion.

These themes are interpreted within the context of Andalusian society and its historical transformations.

Ibn Rushd (Averroes)

Ibn Rushd is portrayed as the culmination of Arab-Islamic rationalism.

Key themes include:

The autonomy of reason.
The defense of philosophy.
The harmony of philosophy and Islamic law.
Aristotelian interpretation.

According to Khalifa, Ibn Rushd represents the highest stage attained by classical Arab philosophy prior to its historical decline.

Intellectual Conclusion of Part III

This volume demonstrates how Arab philosophy progressed:

From translation,
To theoretical construction,
And ultimately to mature rationalism in the thought of Ibn Rushd.
Part IV: The Development of Modern Arab Thought

The fourth volume examines the formation of modern Arab philosophy in Egypt and the wider Arab world from the nineteenth-century Nahda (Renaissance) to contemporary intellectual movements.

If Part III concludes with Ibn Rushd, Part IV begins after centuries of relative intellectual discontinuity.

Muhammad Abduh

Muhammad Abduh serves as the starting point.

Khalifa analyzes:

Religious reform.
The reopening of ijtihad.
The reconciliation of Islam and modernity.
Critiques of intellectual stagnation.

Abduh is portrayed as the intellectual father of the modern Arab renaissance.

Arab Reformers and Nahda Thinkers

The discussion includes figures such as:

Jamal al-Din al-Afghani.
Farah Antun.
Shibli Shumayyil.
Qasim Amin.
Abd al-Rahman al-Kawakibi.

Their positions are examined with respect to:

Freedom.
The state.
Science.
Social reform.
Relations with the West.
The Emergence of Academic Philosophy

Khalifa traces:

The rise of philosophy as an academic discipline.
New rationalist studies.
Projects aimed at renewing and reinterpreting Arab heritage.

He analyzes the transition from religious reform to specialized philosophical inquiry.

Yusuf Karam

Particular attention is given to Yusuf Karam's role in:

Writing the history of philosophy.
Establishing academic philosophical discourse in Arabic.
Presenting philosophical heritage through modern scholarly methods.
Zaki Naguib Mahmoud

Khalifa devotes significant attention to Zaki Naguib Mahmoud as one of the most influential Arab philosophers of the twentieth century.

The discussion focuses on:

Logical positivism.
Scientific rationality.
The critique of superstition.
The relationship between heritage and modernity.

He is presented as one of the most important advocates of intellectual modernization in the Arab world.

Contemporary Arab Thought

The analysis extends to contemporary intellectual debates concerning:

The crisis of the Arab renaissance.
Modernity.
Democracy.
Heritage.
Identity.

Khalifa argues that modern Arab thinkers continue to confront many of the same fundamental questions that emerged during the formative periods of Islamic thought, albeit under radically different historical conditions.

The General Intellectual Structure of the Series

The entire project may be summarized as a four-stage history of Arab consciousness:

Part I: How did the society that produced Islam emerge?
Part II: How did theological schools and Islamic intellectual traditions arise?
Part III: How did Arab philosophy develop and reach its culmination in Ibn Rushd?
Part IV: How did modern Arab thinkers attempt to revive the project of rationality and renaissance?

In essence, the series constitutes a comprehensive intellectual history of the evolution of Arab consciousness from the pre-Islamic period to contemporary Arab thought.

The Influence of Marxism on Abdullah Khalifa's Thought

Although Khalifa cannot be described as an orthodox Marxist in the strict ideological sense, Marxism and historical materialism clearly function as major intellectual influences within his work.

The influence is visible in several ways:

1. Explaining Ideas Through Society

Khalifa consistently investigates:

Social classes.
Economic structures.
Political conflicts.
State formation.

Rather than treating ideas as isolated entities, he seeks to explain the historical conditions under which they emerged.

2. Connecting Thought and Social Reality

When analyzing movements such as:

The Kharijites,
The Mu'tazilites,
The Isma'ilis,
Al-Ghazali,

he does not limit himself to textual analysis but examines the social and political conditions that produced these intellectual currents.

3. Emphasis on Historical Conflict

History, in Khalifa's interpretation, advances through:

Social contradictions.
Political struggles.
Economic transformations.

This emphasis reflects a methodology strongly influenced by historical materialism.

4. Influence of Hussein Muruwwa

Khalifa's project resembles that of Hussein Muruwwa in its attempt to write the history of Arab thought through a historical-social perspective that links ideas to their conditions of production.

Is Abdullah Khalifa an Orthodox Marxist?

The evidence suggests that he is not.

He:

Takes religion and philosophy seriously as intellectual phenomena.
Does not reduce Islamic thought to economic determinism.
Grants philosophers and theologians a degree of intellectual autonomy.
Seeks to understand Islam historically rather than dismiss it as a mere ideological reflection of material conditions.

A more accurate characterization would therefore be:

Abdullah Khalifa is an Arab intellectual who employs a historically and socially informed methodology influenced by Marxism in order to interpret Arab-Islamic heritage, rather than an orthodox Marxist theorist in the traditional ideological sense.

For this reason, the central question underlying his entire project is:

How did religious and philosophical ideas emerge from the historical development of Arab and Islamic society?

This question remains the organizing principle of Idealist Trends in Arab-Islamic Philosophy and the foundation of Khalifa's broader intellectual enterprise.

Short Summary

Idealist Trends in Arab-Islamic Philosophy is Abdullah Khalifa's major intellectual project, which traces the development of Arab and Islamic thought from pre-Islamic Arabia to the modern era.

Rather than studying philosophy and religion as isolated ideas, Khalifa connects them to the social, economic, and political conditions in which they emerged. The series is divided into four main stages:

Pre-Islamic Arabia and the rise of Islam: Islam is examined as both a religious and a historical transformation that helped unite Arab society.
The emergence of Islamic sects and theology: Movements such as the Mu'tazilites, Shi'a, and Kharijites are analyzed in relation to social and political conflicts.
The development of classical Islamic philosophy: Major thinkers including Al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, Al-Ghazali, and Ibn Rushd are studied, with Ibn Rushd presented as the peak of Arab-Islamic rationalism.
Modern Arab thought: The work explores reformers and modern philosophers such as Muhammad Abduh and Zaki Naguib Mahmoud and their attempts to reconcile tradition with modernity.

A key feature of Khalifa's approach is its historical and social perspective, influenced by Marxist and historical-materialist methods. However, he is not an orthodox Marxist. Instead, he uses these tools to answer a central question:

How do social and historical conditions shape the emergence and development of religious and philosophical ideas?

In essence, the series offers a comprehensive history of the evolution of Arab intellectual consciousness from the pre-Islamic period to contemporary Arab thought.

Abdullah Khalifa (1948–2014)
Bahraini Writer, Novelist, and Intellectual

Abdullah Khalifa Al Buflasa (1 March 1948 – 21 October 2014) was a Bahraini novelist, short-story writer, journalist, and intellectual known for his contributions to modern Arabic literature, cultural criticism, and political thought. He was regarded as one of Bahrain's most prolific contemporary authors, producing works in fiction, literary criticism, intellectual history, and social analysis.

Early Life and Education

Khalifa was born on 1 March 1948 in the Gudaibiya district of Manama, Bahrain. He received his education at the Bahrain Teachers' Institute, where he trained as a teacher.

Following his graduation, he worked in the educational sector before dedicating much of his life to literary, cultural, and intellectual activities.

Political and Intellectual Engagement

During the 1960s, Khalifa joined the National Liberation Front of Bahrain, one of the country's leading leftist political movements. His political activism led to his arrest in 1975, and he was subsequently dismissed from his teaching position. He remained imprisoned until 1981, after which he resumed his cultural and journalistic activities.

His political experiences had a significant influence on his literary and intellectual production, particularly his interest in issues of social justice, freedom, political authority, and historical change.

Literary Career

Khalifa began publishing short stories and literary works in the late 1960s. Over the following decades, he established himself as one of Bahrain's most productive authors.

His work encompasses:

Short-story collections
Novels
Literary criticism
Intellectual and philosophical studies
Historical and political essays
Newspaper columns

He regularly contributed to Bahraini and Arab newspapers and journals and became especially well known for his writings in Akhbar Al Khaleej and other cultural publications.

Professional and Cultural Activities

Khalifa was a member of the Arab Writers Union in Syria and participated in numerous literary and intellectual conferences throughout the Arab world.

Among his notable conference contributions were papers on:

The development of the Bahraini short story
Violence in contemporary Arab society
The relationship between intellectuals, freedom, and political authority

Following his release from prison in 1981, he remained active in Bahraini and Gulf cultural journalism, publishing articles, studies, and literary criticism on a regular basis.

Major Literary Works

Khalifa authored dozens of works of fiction, including:

Pearls (1982)
The Pirate and the City (1982)
A Song of Water and Fire (1989)
Fog (1994)
Sea Anthem (1994)
Springs (1998)
The Hour of the Appearance of Spirits (2004)
Omar Ibn Al-Khattab: A Martyr (2007)
Othman Ibn Affan: A Martyr (2008)
Ali Ibn Abi Talib: A Martyr (2008)
Gone with Oil (2010)
Antara Returns to Arabia (2011)
Fatal Punishment (2012)
Rape of a Planet (2014)

He also published numerous collections of short stories, beginning with Winter's Melody (1975) and continuing throughout his career.

Intellectual and Critical Writings

Alongside his creative work, Khalifa produced an extensive body of critical and philosophical studies.

His most important intellectual work is the multi-volume series:

Idealist Trends in Arab-Islamic Philosophy

This project represents an ambitious attempt to examine the historical development of Arab and Islamic thought from a social and historical perspective.

The series includes:

Volumes I–II (2005): The social and intellectual foundations of Islam and early Islamic thought.
Volume III (2005): Classical Arab-Islamic philosophy from Al-Farabi to Ibn Rushd.
Volume IV (2015): The evolution of modern Arab thought.

Among his other notable studies are:

Naguib Mahfouz: From the Historical Novel to the Modern Novel (2007)
Narrative Models from the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula (2008)
The Conflict of Sects and Classes in the Arab Mashriq and Iran (2016)
The Evolution of Arabic Literary Genres (2016)
A New Reading of Marxism (2016)
Lenin and the Adventure of Socialism (2023)
Ideology (2023)
Intellectual Orientation

Khalifa's intellectual approach combined literary creativity with historical and social analysis. While influenced by Marxist and historical-materialist methodologies, he developed an independent framework for understanding Arab-Islamic civilization.

His writings consistently explored:

The relationship between society and ideas
The development of religious and philosophical thought
Social and political transformation
Arab cultural identity
Modernity and intellectual renewal
Death and Legacy

Abdullah Khalifa died in Manama on 21 October 2014 at the age of sixty-six and was buried in Bahrain.

His death was widely mourned within Bahraini and Gulf cultural circles. The Bahrain Journalists Association described his passing as a major loss to Gulf and Arab cultural life, recognizing his extensive contribution to literature, criticism, journalism, and intellectual thought.

Today, Abdullah Khalifa is remembered as one of Bahrain's most prolific modern writers and as an important voice in contemporary Arab literary and intellectual history.
